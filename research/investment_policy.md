
# Active Equity Portfolio Lab
## Investment Policy Statement

**Version:** 1.0
**Portfolio Type:** Hypothetical Active Equity
**Initial Capital:** $100,000
**Benchmark:** S&P 500 Total Return Index
**Investment Horizon:** 3–5 years

---

## 1. Investment Objective

The portfolio seeks to outperform the S&P 500
Total Return Index over a full market cycle
through fundamental security selection and
quantitative portfolio construction.

The strategy emphasizes financially strong
companies trading at attractive valuations,
subject to diversification and risk constraints.

Outperformance is an objective, not a guarantee.

## 2. Investment Philosophy

The strategy combines four principles:

1. Fundamental analysis identifies businesses
   with attractive economic characteristics.
2. Valuation analysis estimates intrinsic value
   and potential investment upside.
3. Quantitative factors provide systematic
   evidence for ranking investment candidates.
4. Portfolio optimization balances expected
   returns with diversification and risk.

## 3. Investment Universe

Eligible investments:
- U.S.-listed common stocks
- Companies with sufficient financial disclosures
- Companies with adequate historical price data

Excluded investments:
- Options and derivatives
- Leveraged instruments
- Short positions
- Cryptocurrencies
- Preferred stock

ETFs may be used as benchmarks or analytical
comparators but are not part of the initial
security-selection universe.


## 4. Security Selection

The portfolio uses industry-specific fundamental
valuation analysis to identify securities with
attractive risk-adjusted return potential.

No universal valuation multiple is assumed to
represent overvaluation.

### 4.1 Industry-Specific Valuation

Companies are compared against historical and
current valuation multiples within their
respective industries.

Primary valuation metrics:
- Price-to-Sales (P/S)
- Enterprise Value-to-Sales (EV/Sales)
- Price-to-Earnings (P/E)
- Enterprise Value-to-EBITDA (EV/EBITDA)
- Free Cash Flow Yield

Historical valuation thresholds will be
investigated using industry distributions
and subsequent stock performance.

### 4.2 Growth Analysis

Company growth will be evaluated relative
to expected industry growth.

Metrics include:
- Year-over-year revenue growth
- Three-year revenue CAGR
- Forward revenue growth estimates
- Industry revenue growth expectations
- Company growth premium vs. industry

Industry growth serves as a forecasting
benchmark, not an automatic company forecast.

### 4.3 Financial Quality

Companies will be evaluated using:
- Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
- Operating Margin
- Free Cash Flow Margin
- Debt-to-Equity Ratio
- Net Debt-to-EBITDA
- Interest Coverage Ratio

Industry-specific differences in capital
structure and profitability will be considered.

### 4.4 Historical Threshold Research

The strategy will investigate whether
elevated valuation multiples predict:

- Lower future stock returns
- Greater downside risk
- Valuation multiple contraction
- Underperformance relative to industry peers

Forward performance periods:
- 3 months
- 6 months
- 12 months
- 24 months

Thresholds will be identified using
historical observations and tested
on later, out-of-sample periods.

### 4.5 Investment Decisions

Valuation multiples serve as research
signals, not automatic buy/sell rules.

Final security selection requires:
- Industry-relative valuation analysis
- Fundamental company research
- Revenue and earnings forecasts
- Financial risk assessment
- Documented investment thesis

The portfolio will only use information
available at each historical decision date
when evaluating strategy performance.

## 5. Portfolio Construction

Initial portfolio constraints:

- Target holdings: 15–20
- Maximum initial security weight: 10%
- Maximum sector weight: 30%
- Cash allocation: 0–5%
- Long-only positions
- No leverage

Allocation methods to evaluate:

1. Equal weighting
2. Minimum-variance optimization
3. Mean-variance optimization
4. Risk-aware constrained optimization

Infeasible constraints must be identified
rather than silently relaxed.

## 6. Portfolio Rebalancing

The portfolio will be reviewed quarterly.

Additional reviews may occur following
material changes to a company's fundamentals
or violations of portfolio risk limits.

Trades must be documented, including
investment rationale and transaction costs.

## 7. Risk Management

The portfolio will monitor:

- Annualized volatility
- Maximum drawdown
- Portfolio beta
- Tracking error
- Sector concentration
- Security concentration
- Factor exposures
- Turnover

Risk limits will be reviewed as research
and backtesting progress.

## 8. Performance Measurement

Portfolio performance will be compared
against a consistent total-return benchmark.

Primary evaluation metrics:

- Annualized total return
- Annualized volatility
- Sharpe ratio
- Information ratio
- Maximum drawdown
- Tracking error

Results must distinguish historical backtests
from forward-tracked paper performance.

## 9. Research Integrity

Backtesting must use information available
at each historical decision date.

The research process should address:

- Look-ahead bias
- Survivorship bias
- Transaction costs
- Corporate actions
- Missing data
- Model uncertainty

All assumptions and material methodology
changes must be documented.

## 10. Portfolio Reporting

The portfolio will publish:

- Holdings and allocation summaries
- Security research and investment theses
- Portfolio performance
- Risk and factor exposure analysis
- Rebalancing decisions
- Methodology and limitations

This portfolio is for educational and
research purposes only.
